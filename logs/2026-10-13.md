## 💡 Concept of the Day: Webhooks — APIs in Reverse

**The one-line version:** Instead of your app *asking* a service "anything new?", webhooks let the service *call you* the moment something happens — HTTP callbacks, push instead of poll.

**The idea:** Polling is a needy customer asking the shop "is my order ready?" every 5 minutes. A webhook is the shop texting you when it ships. When a payment completes, a push lands, or a PR merges, the provider sends an HTTP POST to a URL you registered, with the event as JSON.

**Three things that matter in production:**
- **Verify the signature** — every serious provider (Stripe, GitHub, Razorpay) signs the payload with HMAC. Check it before trusting the body — an unverified webhook is just a random POST from the internet.
- **Answer fast, process later** — reply 2xx immediately and handle the work in a background queue. Slow handlers trigger provider retries, and retries mean duplicates.
- **Expect duplicates** — design handlers to be idempotent: dedupe on the event ID the provider sends (see the [Oct 7 log on idempotency keys](2026-10-07.md)).

## 🛠️ Tool of the Day: ngrok — Test Webhooks on Your Localhost

**What it is:** ngrok gives your laptop a public HTTPS URL and tunnels traffic back to your local server — so Stripe, GitHub, or Razorpay can reach your `localhost:3000` while you develop. [ngrok.com](https://ngrok.com)

**Why it's useful:**
- Webhooks require a publicly reachable URL — ngrok removes the "deploy to a server just to test" step
- The inspection dashboard at `localhost:4040` shows every request's headers and body, with a **Replay** button to re-fire an event without reproducing it
- The free tier includes a static domain, so you don't re-register the webhook URL every restart

**How to try it:**
```bash
# one-time auth (grab your token from the ngrok dashboard)
ngrok config add-authtoken <your-token>

# expose your local server
ngrok http 3000
# → Forwarding: https://your-domain.ngrok-free.app -> http://localhost:3000
```
Paste that URL plus your `/webhook` route into the provider's dashboard, trigger a test event, and watch it hit your laptop in real time.
